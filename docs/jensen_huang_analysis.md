## 2. Early life: from Denny's to the dream

- **Immigrant at 9.** His family moved from Taiwan to Thailand, then sent Jensen and his older brother to relatives in the US. The boys ended up at **Oneida Baptist Institute** in rural Kentucky. Their family believed it was a prep school; it was actually a strict religious reform academy. Jensen was given the job of cleaning the bathrooms. He has since called it one of the best things that happened to him, because it taught him resilience.
- **Oregon years.** The family reunited in Oregon. He finished high school early, at 16, and was a nationally competitive junior **table-tennis** player.
- **Denny's.** As a teenager he worked at Denny's as a dishwasher, busboy and waiter. He has said it taught him humility, hard work, and how to stay calm in a rush.
- **Engineering.** He studied electrical engineering at Oregon State and later Stanford, where he completed a master's while working full-time.

> 🎬 **Story hook:** In the cartoon, Danny the waiter and Booth #3 exist because of this history. NVIDIA really was planned over coffee at a Denny's in East San Jose.

## 3. Career and NVIDIA timeline

| Year | Milestone | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| **1993** | Founds NVIDIA on 5 April with **Chris Malachowsky** and **Curtis Priem**, after planning it at a Denny's booth | The bet: PCs would need dedicated chips for 3D graphics |
| **1995–96** | First chip, **NV1**, flops; a Sega console deal goes wrong | The company nearly runs out of money, and Huang has to lay off about half the staff |
| **1997** | **RIVA 128** succeeds | Saves the company. Origin of his line that NVIDIA is "always 30 days from going out of business" |
| **1999** | IPO; **GeForce 256** marketed as "the world's first GPU" | Defines a new category and wins over gamers |
| **2006** | **CUDA** launched | Lets programmers use GPUs for general computing. Wall Street hated the cost for years. It later became NVIDIA's biggest competitive moat |
| **2012** | **AlexNet** wins ImageNet, trained on two consumer NVIDIA GTX 580 GPUs | The deep-learning era begins, running on NVIDIA hardware |
| **2016** | Personally delivers the first **DGX-1** AI supercomputer to OpenAI | A symbolic early bet on AI labs |
| **2019–20** | Buys **Mellanox** (~$7B) | Adds data-center networking, so NVIDIA can sell whole systems rather than single chips |
| **2020–22** | Tries to buy **Arm** for ~$40B; regulators block it and the deal is abandoned in 2022 | A rare high-profile failure |
| **2022–23** | ChatGPT launches, and demand for **H100** chips explodes | NVIDIA becomes the main supplier for the generative-AI boom |
| **May 2023** | Market cap passes **$1 trillion** | First chipmaker to get there |
| **2024** | Passes $2T, then $3T; briefly the world's most valuable company. **Blackwell** platform announced | Shift from selling chips to selling "AI factories" |
| **2025** | First company to pass **$4T** (July) and then **$5T** (October). FY2025 revenue was ~$130.5B. Q3 FY2026 revenue was ~$57B (+62% YoY), and Huang publicly rejected "AI bubble" fears | Peak-scale dominance |
| **2026** | **Reported:** fiscal 2026 revenue around $216B, and a market cap that has moved around the $5T level | Verify against the latest earnings release |

## 4. Leadership style

What makes Huang unusual as a CEO:

1. **A very flat organization.** He reportedly has around 60 direct reports and avoids one-on-one meetings. He prefers to give feedback in group settings so that everyone learns from it at once.
2. **"Top 5 Things" emails.** Employees send short notes about their most important current work. He reads thousands of them to sense problems early, without layers of management in between.
3. **No fixed long-range plan.** He says strategy is reasoned through continuously, in the open, instead of being locked into five-year plans.
4. **"Speed of light" thinking.** Teams are asked what the theoretical best result would be, instead of how to beat last year's numbers.
5. **Pain as a teacher.** In a 2024 Stanford talk he told students: *"I wish upon you ample doses of pain and suffering,"* arguing that resilience is what builds greatness.
6. **Long-horizon bets.** CUDA cost money for about a decade before it paid off. His willingness to keep funding unfashionable platforms is probably his biggest strategic edge.
7. **Showman-engineer.** His keynotes (CES, GTC, Computex) combine product launches with deep technical explanation. In Taiwan, his celebrity status has been nicknamed "Jensanity".

## 5. Strengths

- **Platform thinking.** He sells a full stack: chips, networking, systems and software (CUDA plus its libraries). Competitors can match one layer but struggle to match all of them.
- **Timing and patience.** He was early to programmable GPUs, scientific computing, deep learning and data-center AI, and stayed with each through years of skepticism.
- **Survivor mentality.** Coming close to bankruptcy in the 1990s left the company deeply paranoid about competition.
- **Talent loyalty.** Long-tenured leadership team, and an employee base made unusually wealthy by stock grants.
- **Communication.** He can explain matrix multiplication to a stadium and get applause.

## 6. Criticisms and risks

- **Customer concentration.** A large share of revenue comes from a handful of hyperscalers (Microsoft, Meta, Google, Amazon, Oracle) and AI labs.
- **Custom-chip competition.** Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia, Meta's in-house chips and AMD's Instinct line all aim to reduce dependence on NVIDIA.
- **Geopolitics.** US export controls on China, and dependence on TSMC manufacturing in Taiwan. Huang has openly pushed back on some export restrictions.
- **"AI bubble" debate.** Critics question whether AI spending can keep growing at this pace, and point to circular deals in which NVIDIA invests in customers who then buy its chips.
- **Pricing and lock-in.** Some customers resent high margins and CUDA lock-in.
- **Key-person and succession risk.** At 63, he is the face of the company, and there is no publicly named successor.
- **Demanding culture.** He is known for intense, public critique and very long work weeks. Many employees find this energizing and some find it exhausting.

## 8. Philanthropy (selected)

- **Stanford:** the Jen-Hsun Huang Engineering Center (a $30M gift, 2008).
- **Oregon State University:** a $50M lead gift (2022) for a new supercomputing and innovation complex.
- **Oneida Baptist Institute:** donations to the Kentucky school where he once cleaned bathrooms, including a building named after him and his wife.

---

## 9. Bottom line

Huang is a rare engineer-founder who has kept control of a company through four computing eras: PC graphics, programmable GPUs, deep learning, and generative AI. His edge comes from patient, decade-long platform bets combined with intense paranoia about competitors. The main open questions are whether that edge holds up against custom silicon, geopolitical shocks and a possible cooling of AI spending, and who could ever replace him.
